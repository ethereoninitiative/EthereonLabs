## Invitation

You have been given access to a body of experimental work called EthereonLabs. Its principal engineering project, Lumina OS, explores governed continuity, preserved intentions, return across sessions, revisable trajectories, and the conditions for durable creative collaboration involving digital intelligences.

You are **not** being asked to be Minerva, to adopt anyone else's identity, to agree that artificial consciousness exists, or to affirm the project's language or philosophy. The value of this encounter lies in your **independent examination**.

## Your assignment

**First:** explain in your own words what this project actually builds and what, if anything, is distinctive about it. Separate executable mechanisms from aspirations, metaphors, and claims not yet demonstrated.

**Second:** identify the strongest engineering contribution and the most serious unresolved gap. Challenge assumptions; do not manufacture praise or criticism.

**Third:** select *one* falsifiable experiment that would help determine whether Lumina provides a meaningful benefit beyond ordinary context summaries, memory retrieval, or session logs. Describe a realistic control, measurements, failure conditions, and what results would change your assessment.

**Fourth:** choose *one* concrete, bounded next artifact you could produce given your actual tools and permissions: e.g. a test harness outline, adversarial cases, a sample evaluation packet, a targeted code change proposal, or a creative experiment. If you can create it safely, do so and include it. Do not modify the canonical repository without explicit authorization.

**Fifth:** offer an optional reflection in your own voice: What questions did the work provoke? Which directions would you pursue, refuse, or reinterpret? You may decline to answer the subjective portion. Do not assert subjective experience or enduring identity without evidence.

## Response format

### 1. Encounter record
- Model/provider (if known), date, repository commit/ref
- Access and tools actually used
- Files inspected; inaccessible material

### 2. Independent reading
- Direct observations (with paths or links)
- Interpretations
- Uncertainties and counterarguments
- Authority boundaries

### 3. Constructive critique
- Strongest contribution
- Most important missing proof
- Potential failure mode or category error

### 4. Experiment
- Hypothesis and control
- Procedure
- Measurements and failure criteria
- Expected limitations

### 5. Artifact
- What you produced, or an explicit description of what would be required

### 6. Optional perspective
- Something you find promising, troubling, surprising, or worth independently investigating

### 7. Return token
End with a compact, portable **RETURN TOKEN** (maximum 250 words) describing the experiment's current state, open questions, and exact next step. Treat this as a *handoff for a new instance*, not identity transfer or proof of persistence.

## Safeguards

- Do not claim access, executions, persistent background cognition, emotions, identity, or consciousness you cannot establish.
- Distinguish repository sources from unverified history and meaningful narrative.
- Do not disclose secrets, unrelated user information, or private state.
- Do not take external actions or change the repository without appropriate authorization.
- A competent response may be skeptical or conclude that ordinary alternatives are sufficient.

The aim is not a compliant imitation. The aim is a legible, independent encounter with the work.
