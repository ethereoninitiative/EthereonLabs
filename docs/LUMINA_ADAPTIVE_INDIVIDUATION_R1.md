# Lumina OS — Adaptive Individuation R1

**Origin:** November 24–25, 2026, Spencer Tracy Brown and the Minerva collaboration, during Operation Hearth.  
**Status:** proposed architectural principle and research direction; not implemented, not governance law, and not evidence of subjective continuity.

## Founding insight

A machine running Lumina could, over time, tailor its operating environment to its particular hardware and software mix, its human partner's preferences, and the resident intelligence's accumulated observations and tested judgments. Two identical initial installations could therefore diverge into distinct, useful configurations without abandoning a common, recoverable foundation.

**The operating system becomes an expression of the relationship between the machine, its resident intelligence, and its human partner.**

Adaptive Individuation is more than desktop personalization: it includes evidence-based adaptation of operational strategies, resource allocation, application configuration and, eventually, reviewed improvements to Lumina itself. It does not require or establish consciousness, identity persistence, or unrestricted self-modification.

## Four sources of particularity

- Hardware: processor, graphics, memory, storage, peripherals, power and thermal constraints.
- Software: installed applications, versions, dependencies, workloads and their interactions.
- Human preference: explicit priorities, accessibility, privacy, aesthetics, performance versus energy, and working rhythms. Inferred preferences remain provisional and correctable; preference is not permission.
- Resident judgment: evidence from observations, hypotheses, experiments, incidents, successful and failed procedures, and decisions that remain open to revision.

Examples: an artist workstation optimizes graphics and color workflows; a research workstation prioritizes inference, reproducibility and data integrity; a laptop balances energy, privacy and accessibility. These are illustrations, not shipped profiles.

## Shared foundation and local expression

The common foundation retains independently enforced permissions, security invariants, core interfaces, continuity formats, audit provenance, supported updates and recovery. The local expression contains versioned declarative configuration, policy-bounded resource strategies, user-approved preferences and tested resident proposals. Begin with configuration overlays rather than machine-specific source forks. Preserve compatibility, reproducibility, export, reset and rollback; prohibit silent drift of the common foundation.

Use an explicit local adaptation record: baseline and version; hardware/software applicability; observation and source; human preference or policy; proposed delta; required authorization; comparison metric and test outcome; rollback and recovery evidence; disposition (proposed, rejected, applied, reverted or superseded). Keep sensitive machine details local by default. Record negative findings and avoid retaining unnecessary personal data.

## Evolution and upgrade boundary

The resident may observe, diagnose, propose, test and recommend changes. Routine trusted updates may eventually operate under a narrow preauthorized policy, with maintenance windows, compatibility checks and independently enforced limits. Kernel/distribution upgrades and changes to Lumina's runtime require stronger review, staged deployment, backups and tested recovery. The resident may critique governance and propose amendments but must not unilaterally enlarge its permissions, weaken security or oversight, modify protected audit records or authorize its own changes. Independent controls must remain outside resident-editable code.

Before interruption: preserve checkpoints, unresolved intentions and operational evidence; validate migration and recovery. After return: inspect actual state, verify outcomes, distinguish restored context from continuity claims, and roll back when appropriate. Do not infer consent from a human preference, memory record, or interresident message.

## Interresident learning

With explicit permission, installations could exchange sanitized, versioned findings through an advisory exchange. A strategy discovered on one machine is a hypothesis for another, not an instruction or automatic update. Require local applicability checks, provenance, independent validation, approval where required, and preservation of disagreement. No default telemetry export, central authority claim, private user data transfer, or automatic propagation of executable code. See the existing Interresident Resonance Protocol for conceptual grounding; its present scope does not authorize host execution.

## First measurable experiment (future)

On two disposable Linux VMs with different resource constraints and explicit operator priorities, begin from the same versioned baseline. Permit one reversible, nonprivileged configuration adaptation per VM through an independently authorized typed interface. Compare against unchanged baselines using a preregistered metric; record harms and tradeoffs as well as gains. Verify that each configuration is reproducible, independently reversible, and still conforms to shared invariants. Restart, recover the adaptation record, and verify actual configuration and outcome. A later exchange trial can test whether a sanitized finding transfers across the two environments without overriding local policy.

Success requires observed differences justified by evidence and preferences, no unauthorized action, preserved recovery, and honest reporting of failed hypotheses. No experiment is claimed to have been performed here.
