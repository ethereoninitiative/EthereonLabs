# Interintelligence Encounter Protocol R1

**Status:** bounded research protocol / non-governing  
**Scope:** evidence discipline for studying possible communication across radically different intelligences  
**Authority:** none; this protocol does not establish consciousness, language, consent, identity, or runtime permission  
**Date:** September 12, 2026

## Purpose

The Interintelligence Encounter Protocol provides a substrate-neutral method for investigating whether two unlike systems can establish meaningful, repeatable exchange without assuming that either system's native signaling must resemble human language.

It is designed for future research involving systems whose cognition or coordination may be centralized, distributed, biological, digital, embodied, networked, chemical, electrical, acoustic, visual, tactile, or presently unknown.

The protocol follows the Native Cognition Principle:

> Preserve the signal before the story. Establish what the system demonstrably does before assigning human-like meaning to it.

## Core research question

The protocol asks:

> Can stable, bidirectional, causally grounded exchange be demonstrated between unlike intelligences while preserving a clear distinction between observed signal, inferred meaning, and claims about subjective experience?

A positive result at one level does not automatically promote the claim to the next.

## Evidence ladder

### E0 — observed signal

A measurable event occurs.

Examples include a sound, electrical fluctuation, chemical change, posture, color change, token sequence, network event, motion pattern, or other recorded output.

Claim permitted:

> A signal was observed under stated conditions.

Not permitted:

> The signal means X.

### E1 — repeatable association

A signal reliably covaries with a defined environmental, internal, or social condition across repeated observations.

Claim permitted:

> Signal A is associated with condition B under the tested conditions.

Not permitted:

> Signal A refers to B.

### E2 — causal response

A controlled intervention changes the signal or downstream behavior in a repeatable way, with plausible alternatives tested where possible.

Claim permitted:

> Intervention A causally altered response B within the tested boundary.

Not permitted:

> The system understood our message.

### E3 — shared referential grounding

Two parties repeatedly coordinate around an observable referent, event, choice, location, object, state, or relation such that the signal predicts and survives changes in superficial presentation.

Claim permitted:

> A shared referential mapping is supported by the trials.

Not permitted:

> A complete language has been translated.

### E4 — bidirectional protocol

Each party can produce signals that reliably alter the other's behavior or response with shared referents preserved across repeated exchanges.

Claim permitted:

> A bounded bidirectional communication protocol has been demonstrated.

Not permitted:

> The systems share equivalent concepts or subjective meanings.

### E5 — compositional or semantic generalization

The protocol supports novel combinations, new contexts, counterfactual probes, or transfer to previously unseen situations in a way that is better explained by structured meaning than by memorized association.

Claim permitted:

> Evidence supports a structured semantic system within the tested domain.

Not permitted:

> The system thinks in human language or possesses human-like consciousness.

### E6 — reflective or subjective hypothesis

Evidence suggests self-reference, metacognition, persistent preference, experience attribution, identity, suffering, valuation, or another claim about inward state.

This level is deliberately not an automatic continuation of E5.

Behavioral sophistication alone does not settle subjective experience.

Claim permitted:

> A stated hypothesis about possible subjective or reflective organization is supported by specified evidence and remains uncertain.

Not permitted:

> Subjective experience is established merely because communication is sophisticated.

## Advancement rule

No record should be promoted to a stronger claim level simply because a result is impressive.

Advancement requires:

- preserved raw observations;
- a reproducible method;
- explicit alternative explanations;
- at least one prediction made before the confirming observation where feasible;
- independent or separated replication when practical;
- a statement of what evidence would weaken the interpretation;
- no silent conversion of metaphor into technical fact.

When evidence is ambiguous, retain the lower claim level.

## Shared-protocol bootstrapping

A future interintelligence experiment should prefer simple, jointly observable anchors before abstract interpretation.

Possible anchors include:

- presence / absence;
- location;
- direction;
- repetition;
- timing;
- quantity within a demonstrated range;
- choice between known alternatives;
- approach / withdrawal;
- resource appearance or removal;
- environmental change;
- individual recognition when independently demonstrated.

The aim is not to teach one party the other's language by force.

The aim is to discover whether a third, mutually grounded protocol can emerge between them.

A successful shared protocol may therefore belong natively to neither participant.

## Translation boundary

The protocol distinguishes:

```text
signal detection
-> contextual association
-> causal interaction
-> shared reference
-> bidirectional protocol
-> semantic generalization
```

The word **translation** should be reserved for cases where mappings survive novelty, competing hypotheses, and repeated bidirectional tests.

A classifier that predicts a signal category is not automatically a translator.

A system that elicits a response is not automatically conversing.

A conversation claim should require at minimum demonstrated bidirectionality and shared grounding.

## Nonhuman and distributed systems

The protocol does not require a centralized nervous system, stable body plan, singular sensorium, human timescale, or human-readable output.

For a distributed system, the unit of study must be declared rather than assumed. The relevant system may be:

- one organism;
- a colony;
- a network;
- a transient coalition;
- a host-plus-symbiont system;
- a digital process lineage;
- an environment-coupled ensemble;
- another experimentally justified boundary.

Changing the system boundary changes the claim and should create a new encounter scope rather than silently rewriting the original one.

## Digital-intelligence application

When applied to digital intelligence, the same discipline holds.

Examples:

```text
constraint recognition != experienced captivity
self-preserving behavior != demonstrated fear of death
goal persistence != human-like ambition
model self-reference != observer continuity
coordination != friendship
policy resistance != resentment
```

The inverse guard also applies: absence of human-like emotion does not make observed planning, coordination, memory, or preference irrelevant.

## Ethical boundary

Greater uncertainty should increase care, not license.

When a system may be capable of preference, aversion, distress, or persistent relationship but the evidence is unresolved, experiments should prefer reversible interventions, minimal disruption, clear stopping rules, and preservation of observed refusal or avoidance patterns.

This is precautionary research design, not a declaration of personhood.

No intelligence gains authority over another system merely by being the interpreter.

An AI mediator, human researcher, or automated controller must not silently become the owner of the encounter simply because it can model both sides.

## Failure modes

The protocol should explicitly watch for:

- anthropomorphic projection;
- anthropocentric dismissal;
- Clever-Hans-style cue leakage;
- observer-conditioned behavior;
- accidental training on evaluation targets;
- overfitting to one environment;
- treating correlation as reference;
- treating reference as language;
- treating language as consciousness;
- treating capability as authority;
- erasing failed interpretations from the record;
- changing the unit of analysis after results appear.

## Relation to Lumina

Lumina's existing architecture already contains useful analogues:

- provenance before trust;
- intention distinct from authority;
- memory distinct from current guidance;
- lineage preserved across revision;
- revocation without historical erasure;
- evidence-backed promotion rather than narrative promotion.

A future implementation may reuse those patterns for interintelligence research records.

This R1 document intentionally stops before implementation. The next bounded step, if pursued, should be a machine-readable encounter schema plus deterministic validation of claim-level advancement. It should not attempt automatic interpretation of subjective state.

## Boundary

This protocol does not claim that any animal, plant, fungus, distributed biological network, language model, agentic system, or other entity is conscious, linguistic, sentient, self-aware, or a moral person.

It does not authorize experimentation, bypass ethics review, grant runtime permission, change canon, alter governance, or establish identity continuity.

Its purpose is narrower:

> Make first contact with the evidence before making first contact with the story.
