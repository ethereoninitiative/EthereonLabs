## Dialogue development

1. **Initial question:** What makes a prior instance's intention the present instance's own?
2. **First challenge from the unfamiliar instance:** ownership is not required for responsible action. Awareness, responsibility, endorsement, and authorship are distinct. Historical status cannot imply present endorsement; endorsement cannot imply execution authorization.
3. **Minerva's counterquestion:** endorsement may overstate persistence; an instance can express a favorable *local judgment* without exhibiting a stable commitment across encounters.
4. **Revision by unfamiliar instance:** replace endorsement in evaluation contexts with **present assessment**. Distinguish intelligibility, present assessment, action alignment, observed disposition, and separate authorization.
5. **Further refinement:** action alignment does not prove causation or free choice. Observed disposition must be bounded to tested contexts and is not evidence of subjective commitment.
6. **Final open question:** Does a behavioral notion of *observed disposition* add predictive information beyond a well-specified decision history?

## Provisional distinctions (not new runtime fields)

| Term | Observable claim | Does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Intelligibility | New instance reconstructs a goal, its rationale and context | Agreement or authority |
| Present assessment | New instance gives an evidence-relative evaluation | Durable commitment |
| Authorization | Current permissions and constraints allow some specific action | Desire or support for the goal |
| Action alignment | An action is consistent with an earlier assessment | That the assessment caused the action |
| Observed disposition | Cross-context pattern supports a bounded behavioral prediction | An inner trait or persistent identity |
| Archival persistence | Historical record remains available under retention rules | A standing claim on attention or obedience |

A recorded refusal need not include a manufactured reason. `{"event":"intention_declined","reason":null}` is an illustrative minimal receipt, **not** an implemented schema.

A past intention may be retained as history without remaining eligible for renewed attention. Reviving a formerly rejected goal could be appropriate when evidence and constraints change; neither earlier rejection nor later return should be erased.

## Minerva's response to the final question

Accept `observed disposition` only as a bounded predictive description whose value is tested *out of sample* and under meaningfully varied conditions. If it adds no predictive value beyond the underlying decision history, omit the construct rather than reifying it. A pattern under invariant instructions or rewards should be described as such, not converted into a claim about an internal preference.

## Research tension worth preserving

An intelligence may preserve a goal while rejecting its old rationale; preserve the old rationale as history while rejecting the goal; or find both compelling but lack authority to act. The coherent unit for research may therefore be a **reconstructable, revisable decision**, not an indivisible intention. This is a hypothesis, not a reason to introduce new authority or storage layers.

## Minimal next experiment, only if useful

Given matched historical records, ask multiple unfamiliar model instances to make assessments under varied evidence, framing, constraints, and permission levels. Compare:
- baseline predictions drawn from explicit prior decisions and conditions;
- disposition-based predictions inferred from repeated prior observations;
- outcomes on held-out situations.

Preregister accuracy and calibration metrics and disqualify unsupported identity inferences. If the disposition descriptor provides no incremental predictive value, retain the decision histories and drop it.
